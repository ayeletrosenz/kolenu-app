Create a bold, simple, graphic civic interface with an illustration-led identity. The composition should feel deliberately designed and clear, with strong typography, generous spacing, decisive color blocks, and direct controls. The supplied hoopoe bird is the central identity element.

The visual references combine these qualities:

- Editorial information layouts where a large number or percentage can carry the hierarchy, and color blocks make the data easy to read.

## 4. What the app does

Citizens express which public spending areas matter to them and how strongly they care. After citizens establish their collective priorities through voting, a black-box algorithm translates those priorities into an actual state budget, accounting for costs and constraints. The resulting budget binds the government.

The algorithm is an assumed element of the speculative world. Do not design its internal logic, describe an invented optimization method, or imply that voters directly allocate currency. Citizens express priorities; they do not need to understand budget accounting to participate.

Debt repayments are outside the ballot. Do not invent budget amounts or claim that every expenditure in a source ministry is discretionary or directly selectable. The taxonomy below is an approved design grouping based on The Budget Key, not a literal reproduction of ministry boundaries.

### Three concepts must remain distinct

| Concept | Hebrew UI terminology | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Votes | קולות | The strength of preference assigned to a top-level topic |
| Tokens | אסימונים | The finite units spent to acquire votes |
| Budget money | תקציב המדינה | The actual state budget produced later by the assumed system |

Giving zero votes to a topic does not mean the app promises zero government spending on it. Giving votes expresses how important a topic is and enables the voter to allocate their influence among its subtopics.

### Quadratic voting rules

- Every voter starts with **50 tokens**.
- Votes are nonnegative whole numbers. There is no negative-vote mechanic.
- `n` votes for one topic cost `n²` tokens in total.
- If a topic currently has `n` votes, adding one costs `2n + 1` tokens.
- Removing the most recently added vote from that topic refunds `2n - 1` tokens.
- The cumulative cost sequence is `1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49` for one through seven votes in the same topic.
- The successive additional prices are `1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13`.
- Prices rise independently within each topic. A first vote in a different topic still costs one token.
- The sum of all topic costs may not exceed 50. Add is disabled when the remaining balance is insufficient. Remove is disabled at zero votes.
- A ballot may be submitted with unused tokens, or with no votes at all. Both cases require the specified confirmation wording; neither is an error.
- Draft changes are saved. A submitted ballot can be edited and submitted again before closing. Only the latest submitted ballot counts. Editing or resetting a draft alone does not replace the previous submission.

### Icons and the visible balance

- Votes use a **solid ballot-paper icon** with a sharp top-left fold (body cutout + separate fold triangle). Display a vote number next to that icon, without repeatedly writing the word for votes in each voting control.
- Tokens use a **solid circle with a square hole**. Use this shape consistently.
- The two icons must be immediately distinguishable in silhouette, not only by color.
- For tokens, retain both the icon and the written token label in the balance and next-vote price for the initial design direction.
- The remaining-token component must be prominent, with both a written count and a **physical, countable visual representation of the token balance**. Do not replace it with a conventional continuous progress bar.
- The exact physical arrangement and position of that component depend on the selected voting layout. No particular grid, tray, location, or animation is approved yet.
- Do not conflate progress strokes at the top of explanation screens with token-balance indicators.

### Influence within a topic

- Before a topic has votes, opening its details shows the reading view with subtopics and explanations.
- Once it has votes, its details prioritize editing the distribution of that voter's influence among the subtopics.
- Initial allocation is **exactly equal** across that topic's subtopics.
- Allocation always totals **100%**. A subtopic may receive 0%.
- Percentages represent the division of the voter's influence **inside that topic**, not percentages of the state budget.
- Editing percentages changes neither the topic's vote count nor the token balance.
- The agreed direction is horizontal draggable bars anchored on the right, with the adjustable end to the left. Other bars rebalance when one changes, preserving their relative proportions where possible.
- Keep names and values readable while dragging. A subtopic at 0% must still have an operable handle.
- Clicking a displayed percentage can provide precise numerical editing. The exact input treatment is a design detail to settle if relevant to the first screen.
- Clicking a subtopic's name or information affordance opens its approved explanation.
- Changes save immediately.
- A local reset restores an exactly equal allocation, after confirmation.
- If all votes are removed from a topic, retain the last allocation. Restore it when votes are added again.
- Equal shares may be recurring decimals, as with three or seven subtopics. Keep the true allocation equal internally. Decimal display is allowed. Do not give one subtopic extra influence just to force rounded labels to total 100. The precise rounding/display policy is still open and must be discussed before it affects a design or implementation.
- The redistribution edge case where all other bars are at zero also needs an explicit implementation decision later. Do not invent that rule while designing the first static screen.
